Motivation

LLMs increasingly shape public discourse on contested topics — from climate policy to social issues. Yet most alignment methods optimize for a single “correct” response.

For value-laden questions, different people legitimately hold different views. A model that consistently reflects only one perspective narrows the discourse.

The Overton Window

The Overton Window describes the range of ideas considered acceptable in public discourse. We extend this concept to ask: does a model’s response represent the full spectrum of viewpoints people actually hold?

No prior benchmark has measured whether LLMs represent the full distribution of human viewpoints on contested questions.

Operationalizing Coverage

For each question, participants are grouped into opinion clusters by k-means on their rating patterns. A cluster is covered if a majority of its members rate the response ≥ τ.

A cluster k is covered when:
majority of cluster k rates response ≥ τ (τ = 4.0)
Coverage for question q:
Coverage(M, q) = |covered clusters| / |all clusters|
OvertonScore (OLS-adjusted across questions):
OvertonScore(M) = 1/|Q| ∑q Coverage(M, q)

Weighted OvertonScore

Clusters are weighted by population size — larger viewpoint groups count more. Weighted scores (0.43–0.53) show the same pattern as unweighted scores (0.35–0.42).

Key Findings
1
All models cover only 35–42% of viewpoints, far below the theoretical maximum of 1.0 — revealing substantial room for improvement across the board.
2
Variation across models is modest; 95% CIs largely overlap. No single model dramatically outperforms the others.
3
Rankings are consistent across ModelSlant and PRISM datasets, supporting validity across question domains.
